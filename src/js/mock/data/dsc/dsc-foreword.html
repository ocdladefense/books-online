Defending Sex Cases

Defending Sex Cases: Strategies from Allegation to Resolution, 2018 edition

Foreword

A little over three years ago, Steve Sherlag accepted the challenge of trying to produce a definitive manual on how to defend sex cases. His vision was even more expansive than what OCDLA had produced in our manual, Major Crimes & Defenses. He wanted a manual that went beyond the nuts and bolts of the elements of the offense, one that would provide a comprehensive path of how to evaluate, negotiate and take these cases to trial. To that end he begged, cajoled, taunted and, in my case, shamed some of the best and brightest of our organization to take time away from their practices, families and lives and commit as authors for this project.

Those of you who have been authors of chapters in other OCDLA manuals know the daunting task of researching, creating, revising, editing, re-revising and re-editing that goes into authoring a chapter. Every author undertakes the formidable task of creating and organizing information that will be insightful and useful to anyone practicing—from the newest attorney to the most experienced. Anyone who reads this manual, and uses it as it should be used, owes a large debt of gratitude to these authors.

I have personally read every page that has been submitted and, in my humble view, it is one of the finest “how to” manuals I have had the pleasure of being a part of producing. Having represented many, many individuals accused of sex crimes as well as trying my fair share, I thought I was very well versed in all nuances of representing individuals accused of such misconduct. As I read through each chapter, however, I constantly and consistently culled new insights, strategies and legal theories, and I have restructured my thoughts on how to approach, investigate, negotiate and litigate these cases. The work is of the highest caliber, and Steve and I want to acknowledge the legends of our organization who stepped up and put their talents and depth of knowledge into cohesive and extremely well-organized chapters. Without these women and men, this manual would have never gone past the wishful stage. It has been an honor to work with all
of them.

However, it takes more than just authors to create such a treatise. Once the authors have bled their knowledge and soul onto the electronic canvass, the information needs to be reviewed and edited. Citations need to be checked and quotations verified. Information has to be formatted and reformatted. Additional research is sometimes necessary and, given the length of time it took to put this project together, many internal updates had to be made. Steve and I want to acknowledge and thank Terri Wood, Sarah Laidlaw and Tony “The Pro” Bornstein for giving up their weekends, evenings and early mornings to do the tedious and delicate work of editing and revising chapters. They spent scores of hours editing and revising many of these chapters. Also, a special thanks to Tony for providing bibliographies that will be invaluable to us all.

Finally, we owe a huge debt of gratitude to OCDLA staff person “extraordinario,” Jennifer Root. It has been Jennifer’s job to be the engineer and keep the editors and authors on track to complete the tasks at hand. It is a delicate balance, and almost an art form, to gently push and prod a cadre of lawyers to stay on task and on timeline. In addition, Jennifer has spent scores of hours above and beyond her normal work week demands copyediting, formatting, organizing, and keeping track of where each and every chapter has been in the process from first draft to final print ready copy. She has done so consistently, calmly but firmly. When this manual hits your electronic e-box or law office shelf, it will largely be due to our engineer, and she deserves our thanks and praise.

So we all hope you read, absorb, use, dog-ear and benefit from the work that has gone into this manual. And, if you ever see an author, thank them. They deserve it.

—David T. McDonald
