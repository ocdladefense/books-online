Investigators Manual

2010 edition

Locating Witnesses

Janaan Stoll

Introduction

Investigators spend a considerable amount of their time looking for potential witnesses. These quests can be rewarding or frustrating, quick or long-lasting. Some searches become mazelike as we repeatedly hit dead ends. I personally enjoy the creative energy inspired by a search, which I liken to a professional game of “Hide and Go Seek.”

While the majority of our witnesses are not trying to evade us, we have all had situations where it seems as if the witness may be deliberately hiding from us. Their efforts to out-fox us are later confirmed, at the time of a face-to-face meeting, by their incredulous query of “How did you find me?” The following are some methods for locating people that I have used with success.

Gathering Personal Data on Your Witness

Before you begin your search, try to acquire as much reliable personal data as possible about the witness. This information should include their full name, nickname(s), a.k.a.(s), date of birth (or approximate age), last known address and telephone number, physical description, outstanding features, marital status, names of family members, ex-spouses, significant others, friends, present or former employers, usual type of employment, primary language, country or state of origin, educational background, professional associations, criminal history, gang affiliation, social service agency connections, health history, vices, hobbies, favorite locales, vehicle(s), type of housing, military history, financial situation, other states of residence, and more.

Telephone Directories''

Traditionally, the telephone directory, and especially the White Pages, was thego-to book. It can still be a useful tool in obtaining landline phone numbers and addresses. But these days most people use cellular phones. Mobile phone numbers are not included in telephone directories and this is where the Internet comes in handy. For a fee, a number of web sites can provide mobile phone numbers, addresses and other contact information.

See Chapter 6, “Computers and Computer Evidence,” for an in-depth discussion on the use of the Internet for help in locating individuals.

For recently acquired numbers, you will need to contact Directory Assistance. Be sure to check neighboring area codes, even in adjoining states. If your call to Directory Assistance informs you that your witness has an unpublished number, you may be able to subpoena subscriber information from their local or long-distance telephone company.

Local libraries have copies of telephone directories and reverse directories available through their resource center. If you have a phone number but no address, call the local library and they will search the reverse directory for you at no cost.

Frequently you will have little more to go on than a name and approximate age. Unless your source is certain of the spelling of your witness’s name, never assume a traditional spelling. Creativity is the key.

Computer Databases

Computer databases can be of great assistance in locating witnesses. Anywho.com is a free directory service that receives information from Intelius, Inc., and is gathered through public records. There you can often find home addresses and phone numbers as well as links to a witness’s social networking site(s). Westlaw and Lexis are two fee-based search sites which have “People Finder” databases. These databases search public record filings, including professional affiliations, throughout the country. If your witness is a licensed professional and a property or business owner, there is a high likelihood that you will find their home and/or business address listed in the database. People Finder databases also provide historical data for the addresses such as names of other residents or owners.

Lexis can provide a variety of useful information including personal and professional licenses held, potential relatives, and potential neighbors. You may also find neighbors’ telephone numbers, the approximate value of the home, length of residence, and sometimes the person’s telephone number, date of birth, and social security number. Family Members

Your witness may not have a listed number, but a family member sharing the same surname may be listed. I have located many witnesses by this somewhat haphazard method of calling subscribers. Knowing the city of origin of your witness is often helpful since there still may be family members residing there. When you make these calls it helps to be apologetic, since often the person you call is of no relation to the person you are seeking.

Once a family member is located, explain your situation and see if they are willing to pass on your name and telephone number. Let them know you will accept a collect call from the witness. If you do not hear from the witness within a week or so, re-contact the family member. Sometimes by your second call they have communicated with their relative and have permission to pass on contact information to you.

Department of Motor Vehicles

Another quick source of information is the Department of Motor Vehicles. As a professional investigator, you can establish a DMV account through which you can access their registration information. If your witness has contacted the DMV for a driver’s license, identification card, or to register a vehicle, you can obtain their full name, date of birth, height, weight, last address, name changes, status of license, vehicle information including co-owners and lien holders, and driving record. Co-owners of vehicles and lien holders are potential avenues to finding your witness.

Cole Directory

If DMV has provided you with a potential address, you may want to check a Cole Directory to discover a telephone number or the names of other residents at that address. If you are not a Cole subscriber or believe your witness is at an address in a different state, contact the public library reference desk in the appropriate town. Most libraries have Cole or city directories available. In the event that there is only a nonpublished number for the address, or no phone number listed, ask the librarian to provide you with telephone numbers for nearby neighbors or the apartment manager. Sometimes an apartment name is listed, but there is no indication which telephone number is for the manager. Call Directory Assistance to obtain the manager’s number and ask for the number for that specific apartment complex or trailer court. If you have access to old Cole Directories, they can be useful for historical information since people frequently pass on apartments or rental homes to friends or roommates. Your witness may have had a relationship with a prior tenant who is more easily located and may know the whereabouts.

Neighbors

A call to a neighbor may help you determine whether or not a witness is still living in the neighborhood. In the event that your witness has moved, the neighbor may be able to give you leads to other neighbors who have kept in touch with the witness. If time is of the essence, a kindhearted neighbor, especially in a distant town, may be willing to drop off a note for you at the home of the witness. A call to a neighbor can often bear fruitful information, even if the witness is no longer living in the area, such as contact information for the current resident of your witness’s prior abode. The current occupant may be forwarding mail to the witness or is related to the witness in some way. If a landlord exists, the current occupant will have their name and telephone number. A landlord may be willing to provide you with important contact information from the witness’s rental application, such as a parent’s name and telephone number. Even if the witness has left the area, a neighbor may know where they moved, where they had been working, if they were living with others, or if they had had police contacts.

U.S. Postal Service

If you believe you have an address for your witness but have had no luck in communicating with them, the U.S. Postal Service may be of assistance to you. A discussion with their letter carrier may help you determine whether or not the witness is still receiving mail there. In rural areas, UPS drivers are quite knowledgeable of people’s locations and give excellent directions. If the witness has actually moved, the post office may be able to provide you with a forwarding address (if the witness has filled out a card and you have the proper authority to request it). A letter sent to them at their original address will be forwarded unless too much time has elapsed (in which case your letter will be returned to you with a label indicating the witness’s new address).

Others Keeping Tabs on Your Witness

A helpful way to focus your investigation is to determine who else might want to keep tabs on your witness, e.g., probation or parole officers, current or ex-spouses (especially if they share custody of children), parents, military superiors, medical professionals (especially if the person has a chronic illness), employers, creditors, drug and alcohol treatment programs, plasma centers, facilities for street youth, college alumni offices, secretaries, recog officers, group home workers, professional licensing agencies, school truancy officers, attorneys, caseworkers, board chairpersons, editors and anyone who might benefit from their association with the witness.

Elections Offices

You can also check the elections office for the county where you believe your witness is residing. If your witness is a registered voter, the county elections office will be able to provide you with their full name and current address.

Licensing Agencies

If your witness is a professional whose employment requires licensing, check with whichever agency has licensing authority over them to obtain their business address. Often, medical professionals working for a specific hospital will not be listed in the telephone directory. In the case of a retired professional the licensing agency may give you a home address or a last known business address. The retired professional may have sold their practice, but the current owner may be willing to pass on a letter or message to the witness. If the professional has allowed their license to lapse, you may want to contact others working in their former firm or business to see if anyone has maintained contact with them. It may be that your witness is still working in the same profession but in another state and a former coworker would be able to tell you which state or even provide you with their new number. Many trades require union membership. You may be able to send a message to your witness via their union officer. Perhaps your witness is in the military reserves. If so, their commanding officer may be able to locate them for you.

Business Owner/Place of Work

Your witness may be a business owner and can be found via their business address. You can check with the Secretary of State’s Corporation Division for their business filings. Board members also may serve as leads to your witness. Some less than reputable businesses prefer to operate without listed telephone numbers. One such business I discovered was in the same location as an earlier business that had a telephone number listed in Cole’s. The owner had changed the name of the business while retaining the original telephone number but made it an unlisted number. Often other businesses in the area will have each other’s telephone numbers for security purposes. If you know the location of a business and suspect that your witness is the owner, you may want to contact neighboring business owners or employees of other businesses to confirm your suspicions. In some cases they may even assist you by pointing out the individual. The Tax Assessor’s Office will also have the name and address for the owner of the property in question.

If you know your witness’s place of work you can typically leave a message for them to contact you when they go on a break. If you have a physical description or photograph of your witness and an idea of when their shift ends, you may want to meet them at the end of a shift. (When I contact law enforcement officers or emergency room physicians, I let them know that they can call me whenever there is a lull in their shift, no matter the hour.) If you discover that your witness is no longer employed by a certain business, attempt to locate coworkers who are still in touch with the witness. Possibly the witness was fired and, due to their behavior, their disgruntled employer or supervisor is willing to reveal information from the ex-employee’s job application. If the employee works in a specific field or trade, contact similar businesses where they may have found work. If the individual worked in a large company with various shifts of workers, try calling back during other shifts in order to find a person who was friendly with your witness.

Court Records

Your witness may be rumored to be in financial distress or have a reputation for making rash purchases or for gambling. If that is the case, you may want to check bankruptcy records or civil suits. Bankruptcy

records will provide you with a Social Security Number for your witness, the name of their attorney, perhaps the name of their spouse, a list of their creditors (with contact information) and the items purchased. With their Social Security Number you can return to Westlaw’s People Finder database and check credit bureau information. Typically several years’ worth of addresses will appear, with the most recent address at the top of the list. If that address is no longer good, you might contact their bankruptcy attorney to see if the attorney is willing to pass on a message to their former client. The same holds for a civil attorney who may have represented them in a civil action.

Since you are now reviewing court records, check to see if your witness has been through a divorce. If so, the divorce records will provide you with the name and Social Security Numbers of the witness, their ex-spouse and any children they may have had together. Your witness may have been sued for child support and an address will be listed in the filings. You may want to locate their ex-spouse or any grown children for assistance in locating the witness. Their divorce attorney might also be a possible message passer, if the divorce was fairly recent or there have been subsequent hearings.

If your witness has been involved with the Oregon court system, you will have many sources of information available to you. Begin by doing an OJIN search on your witness. Public OJIN terminals are available at courthouses. You can also purchase access to OJIN through the state. An OJIN search will provide you with their full name, address, criminal offenses, civil case information, names of representing attorneys, judges handling their cases, sentences, case numbers, etc. If they have a case pending, contact them through their counsel of record. If the witness is in the process of serving a state sentence, contact the Oregon Department of Corrections in Salem for the location of their institution. For witnesses on supervision, state and federal probation and parole officers tend to be excellent sources of accurate contact information. If the witness is no longer on supervision, you may want to check their most recent court file to glean invaluable information off the recog sheet, such as names and telephone numbers of employers, close friends and family members. If you know which police agency arrested them most recently, you may be able to purchase their latest booking photo. Jail records will also have address and telephone information on the witness which will be helpful if they have been released. If your witness has lived in other states, check with corrections departments, state police records offices and court records departments in those states as well.

If your witness has been prosecuted federally, you will want to check the federal docket available on the public computer in the clerk’s office at the federal courthouse. Again, if there is a case pending, you will need to contact the attorney of record to request access to the witness. If the witness has been sentenced and is being supervised, you can obtain contact information from their federal probation officer. If they are serving a sentence, you will need to have their full name and date of birth in order to call the U.S. Marshals’ inmate locator service, located in Washington D.C., for the name and location of the federal institution in which they are serving their time.

Social Service Agencies and Treatment Centers

Even if your witness is transient or homeless, you may be able to communicate with them via social service agencies. If you suspect that your witness occasionally stays at homeless shelters, you can request that messages be left for them on bulletin boards at the shelters. Agencies that offer counseling, food stamps or medical treatment for the homeless may be willing to accept a letter for your witness to be placed in their file until the witness returns to the agency. (You may want to enclose some change in the letter to facilitate the telephone call from a pay phone.) Security guards and police officers working in or patrolling the area where you suspect your witness might be found may be familiar with your witness and their habits and can give you suggestions on where to find the witness.

Perhaps your witness is an alcoholic and you know where they typically drink. The bartender is most likely familiar with your witness and might be willing to hand the phone over to them if you call when the witness is present. The cashier at a store where the witness purchases their alcohol might also be willing to pass on a message left for the witness. If the witness seems to have disappeared for some time, it is possible that they have entered treatment or have been taken to a detoxification center. Family members or friends of the witness may know the name of the treatment center. Counselors in programs are bound by confidentiality laws that protect their clients’ identities, but a letter sent to your witness at the address of the program will probably be delivered once they have completed their initial “black-out” period. Your witness may be attending Alcoholics Anonymous meetings in the area where you suspect they are living and it would be possible for you to leave them a message on a bulletin board at the meeting place.

If you believe that your witness is an illegal drug user, you might try leaving your messages for them at Narcotics Anonymous sites near where you think the witness is residing. You might also try leaving messages for them at plasma centers, methadone clinics, drug programs (both inpatient and outpatient) and at a social services office where they might be receiving food stamps.

Your witness may have a mental health problem that necessitates counseling and medication. The witness may have spent time in a psychiatric hospital. You may want to try to leave a message for the witness at various mental health agencies. They will not acknowledge your witness’s participation in their program, but a letter to the witness in care of the program might get delivered to them. Your witness may have qualified for Social Security Disability payments and is receiving regular checks. You might be able to send them a letter via the Social Security office in their town by directing it to your witness’s caseworker.

Perhaps your witness has come here from another country. The witness may be connected to social service agencies, churches, stores or restaurants that cater to people from that culture or who speak your witness’s native language. You may want to search these locations in the company of an interpreter. Have the interpreter translate any messages into the witness’s language. You will want to determine in advance if your interview will require the assistance of an interpreter. If the witness does not speak any English, you may want to tell them in your note to schedule an in-person interview with you by calling the interpreter first.

If your witness is quite elderly and no longer living in their home, you may want to check with aging and disabilities services or other agencies such as Meals on Wheels. It is possible that they have gone into an assisted living situation. You may want to call nursing homes in the town where the person was living and simply ask for them. It is possible that they have gone to live with family members elsewhere. You may want to check with former neighbors (in particular, elderly neighbors) who may have known your witness for years and can tell you where they have gone and names of family members. It is also possible to access genealogical records on the Internet or through the Mormon Church if you are searching for family members.

Hobbies and Activities

Think of how your witness chooses to spend their free time. If the witness is a member of a church, you may be able to reach them by contacting the church secretary or by locating a church directory. If they enjoy hunting or fishing, they may have applied for a hunting license or a gun permit. If the witness is a board member for an organization, you may be able to find them at a meeting. If you know which college or university the witness attended, you may contact their alumni office. While an alumni officer will rarely provide you with an address or telephone number (unless you also are an alum of the school), the alumni officer will typically agree to forward a letter from you to the last known address they have. Your witness may belong to a club, volunteer with an agency, or work out at a fitness center. You may be able to leave a message for your witness with the person responsible for the membership list or directory for the organization. They may even be willing to consult their directory and make a call for you. Maybe your witness is a member of a motorcycle club and can be found at a clubhouse that serves the membership or at a specific tavern.

Juvenile Witnesses

If your witness is a juvenile living with a parent or guardian, I would recommend obtaining prior permission to conduct an interview with them from the responsible adult. If you know the student attends a certain school but do not know their address, you may request that a school secretary assist you in making contact with the parent. Most parents appreciate being notified and many wish to be present for the interview. They are also helpful in corralling their child whose schedule they may know.

For an emancipated or homeless juvenile, you may be able to reach them through their juvenile court counselor or juvenile probation officer. Try leaving messages for them with the staff of agencies providing care to youth, such as low-cost medical clinics, youth shelters, residential treatment centers, or with security guards for parks or squares where homeless youth congregate. If you know the youth is a skater, look for them at skating parks or check with other skaters. If the witness is a suspected gang member or associate, you may want to check with gang outreach workers or with programs designed to assist youths in extricating themselves from gang activity.

Sometimes knowing the name of a witness’s child will help lead you to the witness. The family may be involved with DHS, a fact you can confirm by calling the agency to determine the name of the caseworker assigned. In a dependency case, typically the child(ren) and the parent(s) have their own attorneys. The juvenile court records department will be able to give you the name of the attorney assigned to your witness’s case, and through that attorney you may be able to connect with your witness.

Unable to Locate Witness

Suppose you have been attempting to locate your witness for months and have continually hit dead ends. I suggest taking a break from your search, even if it requires that your attorney and client agree to request a set-over. Sometimes if the witness thinks you are no longer looking for them they will suddenly emerge. The witness may have been out of the country, traveling with a circus, following the harvest season to another state, or simply lying low because they have a warrant pending. In time the witness may get arrested, return to the area, or happen to contact one of the individuals with whom you left a message. You may want to re- contact some of your sources. They may have misplaced your name and telephone number.

It can be fun trying to out-smart a witness who seems to be trying to dodge you. If you are fairly sure they are living at a location check back in the evening or early morning; try to catch them as they enter or exit the house. See if any vehicles registered to your witness show up at the house. Run the license plates for other vehicles parked at the house for potential friends of the witness. Be observant. Sometimes there will be a vehicle parked in front of the house with a “for sale” sign in the window, which will provide you with a telephone number!

It is also possible, especially if the person is a potential government witness (such as a snitch), that they have had government assistance in changing their identity and relocating. You may want to ask your attorney to make a motion to force the government to produce the witness. Needless to say, it is much more fun to find them yourself. This is often possible because they tend to continue with illegal activities even while working as an informant.

Finally, if you feel you have exhausted all avenues, you may want to check death records for your witness. Westlaw’s People Finder database has a section for deceased individuals. You might also search newspaper obituary records through Westlaw or call the Vital Statistics Office of the state where you think they were last residing.

Conclusion

Do not give up hope. Talk with other investigators about your efforts. They may have new ideas or suggestions for you. When you finally locate your witness you will have gained their respect. They will undoubtedly know just how persistent you have been in your quest. Hopefully, they will reward you with a thorough interview and their testimony will be of benefit to your client. Of course, there is always the chance that what they know about the case came to them second-hand from their friend “Charlie,” last name and whereabouts unknown…. Good luck!
