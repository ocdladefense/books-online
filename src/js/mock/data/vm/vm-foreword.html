Still at War: A Guide for Defenders, Prosecutors and Judges Dealing with Oregon's Veteran Defendant Crisis

This practitioner’s manual is a product of the June 18, 2015, meeting of the Public Defense Services Commission (PDSC). At that meeting, Dr. William “Bud” Brown (author of Chapter 5) and I presented information explaining societal problems stemming from the frequency of the nation’s veterans who find themselves entangled within the criminal justice system processes.1

Dr. Brown and I further explained that “Oregon has by far and away the highest known veteran imprisonment rate in the nation.”2 We asked the commission to adopt two policy proposals intended to reduce the imprisonment rate by improving the quality of legal representation provided veteran defendants: 1) a proposal to require PDSA-funded providers to adopt and adhere to a best practices model for representing servicemember defendants and 2) a proposal to require that only attorneys who meet minimum qualification standards be allowed to represent servicemember defendants facing felony charges that carry presumptive or mandatory sentences of imprisonment.3

Although the PDSC declined to adopt our policy proposals, consequent to its meeting with Dr. Brown and me, the commission committed to address the state’s veteran imprisonment rate by funding the establishment and operation of a Veterans Defense Resource Center (VDRC). Oregon veterans are indebted to the members of the PDSC for their decision.

The VDRC “provides expert training, support, advice, and assistance to promote zealous representation of veterans and servicemembers in Oregon’s criminal justice system.” James Gardner, who wrote this manual’s Chapter 2 and co-wrote Chapter 12, is the VDRC’s executive director. The VDRC is housed in the offices of the Public Defender Services of Lane County, Inc., in Eugene where Mr. Gardner is a staff attorney.

The announcement of the availability of the VDRC’s web page prompted OCDLA Publications Committee members Sarah Laidlaw and Terri Wood to contact me with a request to produce this manual. Oregon veterans are indebted to Ms. Laidlaw and Ms. Wood for their proposal. Oregon veterans are also indebted to OCDLA Executive Director Shaun McCrea, who has been continuously involved in the manual’s production, and to OCDLA administrative assistant Jennifer Root, who managed the herculean task of producing this manual in time for its release at the 2017 OCDLA Annual Conference.

Finally, I would like to extend my personal thanks to all of the manual’s authors for their excellent work, and to Salem research attorney Jennelle Meeks Barton for her editorial assistance in the production of this manual.

Jesse Wm. Barton, Editor

June 2017
