Certiorari

Eric Johansen

Review in the United States Supreme Courtby means of a writ of certiorari is not a matter of right, but of judicial discretion.1 The Court’s primary concern is not to correct errors in lower court decisions, but to decide cases presenting issues of importance beyond the particular facts and parties involved. The Court grants and hears argument in less than one percent of the cases that are filed each term. The vast majority of petitions are simply denied by the Court without comment or explanation. The denial of a petition for a Writ of Certiorari signifies only that the Court has chosen not to accept the case for review and does not express the Court’s view of the merits of the case.

Petitioning the United States Supreme Court for a writ of certiorari in a criminal case is extremely unusual. For example, Appellate Division of the Office of Public Defense Servicesonly routinely files petitions for certiorari in capital appeal cases. This chapter is intended to provide general information to attorneys to assist with the procedure of filinga petition for a writ of certiorari to the United States Supreme Court. Although some perfunctory information is provided for practice and procedure aftercertiorari is granted, other authorities will need to be consulted to adequately understand and prepare for subsequent briefing and argument upon the granting of certiorari.

Anyone seeking to file a petition for a writ of certiorari, or taking any other action in the United States Supreme Court, should consult the authoritative treatise, Supreme Court Practice, Ninth Edition. It is published by BNA and is the bible for Supreme Court practice.

Also, any practitioner considering filing on an important, cert-worthy issue, should consider obtaining amicussupport from OCDLAor NACDL. Members of each organization’s amicuscommittees are available to discuss issues and the potential for amicussupport. Practitioners should also be sure to solicit others in OCDLA to read their Petition for a Writ of Certiorari or responses for substantive comments and editing.

III. Timeliness of Petition

A petition for a writ of certiorari to review a judgment in a criminal case entered by a state court of last resort is timely when it is filed with the Clerk of the Supreme Court within 90 days after entry of the judgment. SeeRule 13.1.3 {Note: 3 The Clerk will not file any petition for a writ of certiorari that is jurisdictionally out of time. See, e. g., 28 U.S.C. §2101(c). See Rule 13.2.}

The time to file a petition for a writ of certiorari runs from the date of entry of the judgment or order sought to be reviewed, and not from the issuance date of the mandate (or its equivalent under local practice). SeeRule 13.3.

If a petition for rehearing is timely filed in the lower court by any party, or if the lower court appropriately entertains an untimely petition for rehearing or sua sponteconsiders rehearing, the time to file the petition for a writ of certiorari for all parties (whether or not they requested rehearing or joined in the petition for rehearing) runs from the date of the denial of rehearing or, if rehearing is granted, the subsequent entry of judgment. SeeRule 13.3.

For the time and manner of presenting the application, seeRules 21, 22, 30, and 33.2.}

An application to extend the time to file a petition for a writ of certiorari is not favored. SeeRule 13.5.

In the computation of any period of time, the day of the event is not included. If filed less than ten days before the final filing date, such application will not be granted except in the most extraordinary circumstances. SeeRule 30.2.

An application to extend the time to file a petition for a writ of certiorari, to file a jurisdictional statement, to file a reply brief on the merits, or to file a petition for rehearing must be made to an individual Justice and presented and served on all other parties as provided by Rule 22. If denied, such an application may not be renewed. SeeRule 30.3.

IV. Jurisdiction on Writ of Certiorari

Review on a writ of certiorari is not a matter of right, but of judicial discretion. A petition for a writ of certiorari will be granted only for compelling reasons. The reasons the Court considers include when a state court of last resort has decided an important federal question in a way that conflicts with the decision of another state court of last resort or of a United States court of appeals, and/or when a state court has decided an important question of federal law that has not been, but should be, settled by the Supreme Court, or has decided an important federal question in a way that conflicts with relevant decisions of the Supreme Court. SeeRule 10. A petition for a writ of certiorari is rarely granted when the asserted error consists of erroneous factual findings or the misapplication of a properly stated rule of law. SeeRule 10.

Except fora petitioner proceeding in forma pauperis, the petitioner must file 40 copies of a petition for a writ of certiorari, prepared as required by Rule 33.1, and must pay the Rule 38(a)docket fee. SeeRule

12.1.

A petitioner proceeding in forma pauperisunder Rule 39must file an original and 10 copies of a petition for a writ of certiorari prepared as required by Rule 33.2, together with an original and 10 copies of the motion for leave to proceed in forma pauperis. A copy of the motion must precede and be attached to each copy of the petition. SeeRule 12.2.

VI. The Petition

Pursuant to Rule 14.1, a Petition for a Writ of Certiorari must contain (in the following order):

(a) The questions presentedfor review.5 {Note: 5 The questions must be set out on the first page following the cover, and no other information may appear on that page. If the petitioner or respondent is under a death sentence that may be affected by the disposition of the petition, the notation “capital case” must precede the questions presented. SeeRule 14.1.}

(b) A list of all partiesto the proceeding in the court whose judgment is sought to be reviewed.

(c) If the petition exceeds five pages or 1,500 words, a table of contents, and a table of cited authorities.6 {Note: 6 The table of contents must include the items contained in the appendix. SeeRule 14.1.}

(d) Citations of the reports of the opinionsand orders entered in the case.

(e) A concise statement of the basis for jurisdictionin the Court, showing the date the judgment or order sought to be reviewed was entered, the date of any order respecting rehearing, and the date and terms of any order granting an extension of time to file the petition for a writ of certiorari, the statutory provision believed to confer on the Court jurisdiction to review on a writ of certiorari the judgment or order in question, and if applicable, a statement that the notifications required by Rule 29.4(b)have been made.7 {Note: 7 Rule 29.4(b) relates to any proceeding in which the constitutionality of an Act of Congress is drawn into question, and neither the United States nor any federal agency is a party. In such a case, the petition must recite that 28 U. S. C. § 2403(a)may apply and must be served on the Solicitor General of the United States.}

(f) The constitutional provisions and statutesinvolved in the case set out verbatim with appropriate citation.8 {Note: 8 If the provisions involved are lengthy, their citation alone suffices, and their pertinent text must be set out in the appendix. SeeRule 14.1.}

(g) A concise statement of the casecontaining the facts material to consideration of the questions presented, the specification of the stage in the proceedings, both in the court of first instance and in the appellate courts, when the federal questions sought to be reviewed were raised, the method or manner of raising them and the way in which they were passed on by those courts, and pertinent quotations of specific portions of the record or summary thereof, with specific reference to the places in the record where the matter appears (e.g., court opinion, ruling on exception, portion of court’s charge and exception thereto, assignment of error), so as to show that the federal question was timely and properly raised and that the Court has jurisdiction to review the judgment on a writ of certiorari.9 (Note: 9 When the portions of the record are voluminous, they must be included in the appendix. SeeRule 14.1.)

(h) A direct and concise argumentamplifying the reasons relied on for allowance of the writ.10 {Note: 10 SeeRule 10relating to considerations governing review on certiorari.}

(i) An appendixcontaining (in the following order):11 {Note: 11 If the material required by the appendix is voluminous, it may be presented in a separate volume or volumes with appropri ate covers.}

(i) The opinions, orders, findings of fact, and conclusions of law, whether written or orally given and transcribed, entered in conjunction with the judgment sought to be reviewed

(ii) Any other relevant opinions, orders, findings of fact, and conclusions of law entered in the case by the court.

(iii) Any order on rehearing.

(iv) The judgment sought to be reviewed if the date of its entry is different from the date of the opinion or order entered in conjunction with the judgment sought to be reviewed.

(v) The constitutional provisions and statutes involved in the case set out verbatim with appropriate citation if notincluded in the body of the petition.

(vi) The portions of the record when the federal questions sought to be reviewed were raised if not included in the body of the petition.

VII. Additional Considerations Relating to the Petition

All contentions in support of a petition for a writ of certiorari shall be set out in the body of the petition. No separate brief in support of a petition for a writ of certiorari may be filed. The Clerk will not file any petition for a writ of certiorari to which any supporting brief is annexed or appended. SeeRule 14.2.

A petition for a writ of certiorari may not exceed 9,000 words if filed in booklet format, or 40 pages if filed in 8½ x 11–inch paper format. SeeRule 33.

The failure of a petitioner to present with accuracy, brevity, and clarity whatever is essential to a ready and adequate understanding of the points requiring consideration is sufficient reason for the Court to deny a petition. SeeRule 14.4.

If the Clerk determines that a petition submitted timely and in good faith is in a form that does not comply with Rule 14 (Content of a Petition for a Writ of Certiorari), or with Rule 33 (Document Preparation: Booklet Format; 8½ x 11–inch Paper Format), or Rule 34(Document Preparation: General Requirements), the Clerk will return it with a letter indicating the deficiency. A corrected petition submitted no more than 60 days after the date of the Clerk’s letter, and in accordance with Rule 29.2 (detailing general requirements for filing), will be deemed timely. SeeRule 14.5.

VIII. Motion to Proceed in forma pauperis

A party seeking to proceed in forma pauperismust file a motion for leave to do so, together with the party’s notarized affidavit or declaration in compliance with 28 USC §1746, and in the form prescribed by the Federal Rules of Appellate Procedure, Form 4. SeeRule 39.1.12 {Note: 12 An authorized form of an “Affidavit or Declaration” can be found in the online “Guide for Prospective Indigent Petitioners for Writs of Certiorari” on the Supreme Court’s website.}

The motion and affidavit or declaration must be filed together with the petition, and must comply with Rule 21(dealing with motions). An original and ten copies must be filed (unless the party is an inmate confined in an institution and is not represented by counsel, in which case the original, alone, must be filed). A copy of the motion, and affidavit or declaration if required, must precede and be attached to each copy of the petition. SeeRule 39.2.

Except when these Rules expressly provide that a document must be prepared as required by Rule 33.1(Booklet Format), every document presented by a party proceeding under this Rule must be prepared as required by Rule 33.2 (8½ x 11–inch Paper Format). SeeRule 39.3.13 {Note: 13 Unless such preparation is impossible. SeeRule 39.3.}

While making due allowance for any case presented under this Rule by a person appearing pro se, the Clerk will not file any document if it does not comply with the substance of these Rules or is jurisdictionally out of time. SeeRule 39.3.

When the motion and affidavit or declaration are presented to the Clerk, accompanied by proof of service (See Rule 29), they will be placed on the docket without the payment of a docket fee or any other fee. SeeRule 39.4.

If satisfied that a petition for a writ of certiorari is frivolous or malicious, the Court may deny leave to proceed in forma pauperis. SeeRule 39.8.

IX. Filing and Service

Any document required or permitted to be presented to the Court or to a Justice shall be filed with the Clerk. SeeRule 29.1.

A document is timely filed if it is received by the Clerk within the time specified for filing, or it is sent to the Clerk through the United States Postal Service by first-class mail (including express or priority mail), postage prepaid, and bears a postmark, other than a commercial postage meter label, showing that the document was mailed on or before the last day for filing, or it is delivered on or before the last day for filing to a third-party commercial carrier for delivery to the Clerk within three calendar days. SeeRule 29.2.

If submitted by an inmate confined in an institution, a document is timely filed if it is deposited in the institution’s internal mail system on or before the last day for filing and is accompanied by a notarized statement or declaration in compliance with 28 U.S.C. § 1746 setting out the date of deposit and stating that first-class postage has been prepaid. SeeRule 29.2.

If the postmark is missing or not legible, or if the third-party commercial carrier does not provide the date the document was received by the carrier, the Clerk will require the person who sent the document to submit a notarized statement or declaration in compliance with 28 U.S.C. § 1746 setting out the details of the filing and stating that the filing took place on a particular date within the permitted time. SeeRule 29.2.

Any document required to be served may be served personally, by mail, or by third-party commercial carrier for delivery within three calendar days on each party to the proceeding at or before the time of filing. SeeRule 29.3.

If the document has been prepared as required by Rule 33.1(booklet format), three copies must be served on each other party separately represented in the proceeding. SeeRule 29.3.

If the document has been prepared as required by Rule 33.2 (8½ x 11–inch Paper Format), service of a single copy on each other separately represented party suffices. SeeRule 29.3.

If personal service is made, it must consist of delivery at the office of the counsel of record, either to counsel or to an employee therein. SeeRule 29.3.

If service is by mail or third-party commercial carrier, it must consist of depositing the document with the United States Postal Service, with no less than first-class postage prepaid, or delivery to the carrier for delivery within 3 calendar days, addressed to counsel of record at the proper address. SeeRule 29.3.

When a party is not represented by counsel, service must be made on the party, personally, by mail, or by commercial carrier. SeeRule 29.3.

Ordinarily, service on a party must be by a manner at least as expeditious as the manner used to file the document with the Court. SeeRule 29.3.

If the United States or any federal agency is a party to be served, service must be made on the Solicitor General of the United States. SeeRule 29.4(a).

When an agency of the United States that is a party is authorized by law to appear before the Court on its own behalf, or when an officer or employee of the United States is a party, the agency, officer, or employee must be served in addition to the Solicitor General. SeeRule 29.4(a).

In any proceeding in the Court in which the constitutionality of an Act of Congress is drawn into question, and neither the United States nor any federal agency is a party, the initial document filed in the Court must recite that 28 U.S.C. § 2403(a) may apply and must be served on the Solicitor General of the United States. SeeRule 29.4(b).

Proof of service, when required by these Rules, must accompany the document when it is presented to the Clerk for filing and must be separate from it. Proof of service must contain, or be accompanied by, a statement that all parties required to be served have been served, together with a list of the names, addresses, and telephone numbers of counsel indicating the name of the party or parties each counsel represents. SeeRule 29.5.

It is not necessary that service on each party required to be served be made in the same manner or evidenced by the same proof. SeeRule 29.5.

Pursuant to Rule 29, proof of service may consist of any one of the following:

(a) An acknowledgment of service, signed by counsel of record for the party served, and bearing the address and telephone number of such counsel. SeeRule 29.5(a),

(b) a certificate of service, reciting the facts and circumstances of service in compliance with the appropriate paragraph or paragraphs of this Rule, and signed by a member of the Bar of the Court representing the party on whose behalf service is made or by an attorney appointed to represent that party under the Criminal Justice Act of 1964, see 18 U.S.C. § 3006A(d)(6), or under any other applicable federal statute, SeeRule 29.5(b), or

(c) a notarized affidavit or declaration in compliance with 28 U.S.C. § 1746, reciting the facts and circumstances of service in accordance with the appropriate paragraph or paragraphs of this Rule, whenever service is made by any person not a member of the Bar of the Court and not an attorney appointed to represent a party under the Criminal Justice Act of 1964, see 18 U.S.C. § 3006A(d)(6), or under any other applicable federal statute. SeeRule 29.5(c).

X. Disposition of Cases

After considering the documents distributed under Rule 15, the Court will enter an appropriate order. The order may be a summary disposition on the merits. SeeRule 16.1.

Whenever the Court grants a petition for a writ of certiorari, the Clerk will prepare, sign, and enter an order to that effect and will notify forthwith counsel of record and the court whose judgment is to be reviewed. The case then will be scheduled for briefing and oral argument. If the record has not previously been filed in the Court, the Clerk will request the clerk of the court having possession of the record to certify and transmit it. A formal writ will not issue unless specially directed. SeeRule 16.2.

Whenever the Court denies a petition for a writ of certiorari, the Clerk will prepare, sign, and enter an order to that effect and will notify forthwith counsel of record and the court whose judgment was sought to be reviewed. The order of denial will not be suspended pending disposition of a petition for rehearing except by order of the Court or a Justice. SeeRule 16.3.

XI. Case Handling Guides

An online “Guide for Prospective Indigent Petitioners for Writs of Certiorari” can be accessed at the Supreme Court’s website. This guide indicates that it is designed to assist “petitioners who are proceeding in forma pauperisand without the assistance of counsel.” However, it also contains much useful information for attorneys. It provides additional information on various subjects including some of those contained in this manual. It also contains forms such as:

• Motion for Leave to Proceed In Forma Pauperis

• Affidavit or Declaration in Support of Motion for Leave to Proceed In Forma Pauperis

• Petition for Writ of Certiorari

• Proof of Service

Another guide, the “Guide for Counsel in Cases to be Argued before the Supreme Court of the United States,” can also be accessed at the Supreme Court’s website. This guide deals with various nuts and bolts subjects as oral argument, courtroom seating, decisional process, records, and joint appendices and merits briefs.

A brief in opposition to a petition for a writ of certiorari maybe filed by the respondent in any case. A brief in opposition mustbe filed in a capital case (see Rule 14.1(a)), or when ordered by the Court. SeeRule 15.1.14 {Note: 14 SeeRule 15.2 for specifications for filing a brief in opposition.•Rule 21. Motions to the Court'''}

Any time the Attorney General files a Petition for a Writ of Certiorari, counsel should file a Response. Otherwise, the Justices will decide the question with input only from the Attorney General.

Any brief in opposition shall be filed within 30 days after the case is placed on the docket, unless the time is extended by the Court or a Justice, or by the Clerk. SeeRule 15.3.

No motion to dismiss a petition for a writ of certiorari may be filed by a respondent. Any objections to the jurisdiction of the Court to grant a petition for a writ of certiorari shall be included in the brief in opposition. SeeRule 15.4.

A petitioner may file a reply brief addressed to new points raised in the brief in opposition. SeeRule 15.6.

Any party may file a supplemental brief at any time while a petition for a writ of certiorari is pending, calling attention to new cases, new legislation, or other intervening matter not available at the time of the party’s last filing. SeeRule 15.8.
