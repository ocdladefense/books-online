DUII Notebook

It is a privilege to introduce this new edition of OCDLA’s flagship manual, the DUII Trial Notebook. There are some notable changes to the 2020 edition, including a new title: DUII Notebook: A Comprehensive Guide to Your DUII Case — to reflect the broadened scope of applicability that the notebook has morphed into over the years from the original version which contained content exclusively related to DUII trials. In addition to trial preparation aids, the 2020 edition includes information on the intricacies of the DUII statute itself, managing your client while staying sane, the option for DUII diversion, preparing for and winning DMV implied consent license suspension hearings, and appealing a guilty verdict, to name a few.

Much has changed since the last edition came out in 2014. Some seminal DUII cases have modified the landscape, including State v. Banks, State v. Hedgpeth, State v. Beltran-Chavez and State v. Guzman. At the same time, the Oregon legislature, state agencies and law enforcement have been busy counteracting case law favorable to the defense, with new rules to circumvent Banks and a new bill that would undermine Hedgpeth. This notebook covers all the new cases as well as the classics, while providing updates on current legislation. OCDLA will continue to update this notebook as the law evolves so you will always be prepared to best represent your client.

The 2020 edition continues to include the Resource Guide, introduced with the 2014 edition. From the NHTSA DWI Detection and Standardized Field Sobriety Test Participants and Instructors Manuals to the Oregon DUII Diversion Packet and other DMV forms, from the NHTSA DRE Expert Course Manual to the Guide to Implied Consent Hearings and much, much more, you will find all links to these resources in the Master Table of Contents, beginning on page xix.

Driving under the influence of intoxicants is a crime that most people have committed at some point in their lives, and by which many innocent people are caught up in the dragnet of overzealous enforcement. In the age of MADD, federal grants that depend on DUII convictions, treatment centers, ADES and ignition interlock device requirements, driving under the influence is a multimillion dollar industry and we are part of it. Our hard work can help reduce the number of innocent people caught up in the maelstrom of fines, fees, treatment classes, medication management, mandatory abstinence, mugshots and all the other expenses and collateral consequences involved in a DUII arrest.

In 1986, John Henry Hingson III wrote a book called How to Defend a Drunk Driving Case, which was inspired by the original 1982 OCDLA DUII Trial Notebook. DUII defense has now transcended “drunk driving”—prescription medication DUIIs, illegal drug DUIIs and cannabis DUIIs are now very popular with law enforcement. But one thing true in the 1980s is still true today, as John Henry said in the preface to that book:

Each lawyer is a different person, possessed of strengths and weaknesses unique to that individual. If the trial lawyer accepts those strengths and weaknesses with an open heart and mind, there will be greater peace within the person whom the jury sees and hears pleading a client’s case. That inner peace becomes inner strength. That strength produces more victories.

This is why a meditation practice is included in Chapter 2; by training and understanding our minds, we can better understand ourselves and find that inner peace that becomes our strength.

Highlights of the 2020 edition include content written by five new authors. Hugh Duvall contributed a revised and updated “Implied Consent” chapter. Peter Carini reworked the “Field Sobriety Tests” chapter. Justin Morehouse and I created a completely new and thorough chapter covering “DREs and Cannabis.” Anna Sammons and I reworked the chapter on clients to include additional information on client care, working with the addicted client, and taking care of yourself in the process — all reflected in the new title, “You and Your Clients.” The “Pretrial Motions” chapter now contains several don’t-miss sample motions with up-to-date case law. Many thanks to those that contributed pretrial motions for this chapter, including John Henry Hingson, III, Thaddeus Betz, David T. McDonald, Richard Oberdorfer, Justin Morehouse and Hugh Duvall. Shannon Mortimer did a thorough revision of the misdemeanor section of Chapter 1. Kenn Meneely and Don Molnar did a comprehensive update to their already extremely thorough “Blood, Breath and Urine” chapter. Mark Cogan and Jeff Turnoy updated the “Violations and Related Charges” chapter to include several new violations plus related felony charges. Bear Wilner-Nugent completely revised and updated the appeals section to reflect that the ORAPs have been revised considerably in recent years, with appeals becoming even more thoroughly electronic. I would also like to thank my co-editor Ben Eder for contributing his wealth of experience to this notebook and Jennifer Root at OCDLA for doing all of the detailed copyediting, schedule management, design and production to get this document launched in time for the 2020 OCDLA DUII conference in Hood River. 

This 2020 DUII Notebook rests upon the shoulders of the many authors of previous editions and OCDLA thanks the contributions of those previous authors below.

OCDLA first published the DUII Trial Notebook in 1982. The authors of the original edition included John Henry Hingson, III, Helen Bloch, Phil Margolin, Tom Crabtree, John Daugirda, Paul DeMuniz, Rush Hoag, Susan Payden, John Ransom and Bill Uhle. Minor updates occurred in 1984 and 1987. In 1991, Lane Borg and Bill Uhle spearheaded the effort to completely update the DUII Trial Notebook. Contributing to the task were Michael Rose, Garrett Richardson, Rush Hoag, Phil Margolin, John Powers, Steve Verhulst, Cate Wollam, Bob Thuemmel, Joan Peterson, Dianne Denhem and Betsy Chick. OCDLA’s editing and formatting team was John Potter, LaVon Murphy and Sheryl MacDonald.

The fifth edition, released in 1994, also represented a significant update. The Intoxilyzer chapter was rewritten as Oregon’s old Intoxilyzer 4011A had been replaced with the Intoxilyzer 5000. The “Sentencing and Suspensions” chapter underwent major changes. Dozens of new updated DMV forms were included, plus all the new statutes.

The 1997 sixth edition was yet another major update. The contributing authors were Hugh Duvall, Lisa Fraunfelder, Paul Hart, Greg Hazarabedian, Rush Hoag, Lisa Ludwig, David McDonald, Jim McHugh, John Powers, Garrett Richardson, Michael Rose, Mary Shannon, Bob Thuemmel, Steven Verhulst and Cate Wollam. Of particular note was the new “Testing” chapter which incorporated revised information on the Intoxilyzer and new material on field sobriety testing.

In September 2001 the seventh edition was released under the guidance of editors Dave Celuch, Lisa Ludwig and Dana Forman. Contributing authors were Chris Bottoms, Cynthia Bunker, Lisa Fraunfelder, Alexander Hamalian, Andrew Kohlmetz, Tom MacNair, David McDonald, Jim McHugh, John Powers, Garrett Richardson, Michael Rose, Bob Thuemmel, Grant Wenzlick and Cate Wollam.

The eighth edition in 2004 was skillfully edited by Bill Uhle, and you will continue to find many of his “Editor’s Notes” in the chapters. Contributing authors that year were: M. Christian Bottoms, Dave Celuch, Dianne Denhem, John Gilroy, Alexander Hamalian, Andrew Kohlmetz, Lisa Ludwig, David T. McDonald, Jeff Napoli, Richard Oberdorfer, Garrett Richardson, Bruce Tarbox, Bob Thuemmel, Grant Wenzlick and Todd Wilson.

The ninth edition in 2008 saw Cate Wollam at the helm, ably assisted by Spencer Hahn. New chapters that year: “DMV Hearings” by Ben Eder, “Boating Under the Influence (BUII)” by Mark Gorski and John Powers, and “Biking Under the Influence” by Shannon Mortimer. In addition, a section on the Intoxilyzer 8000 was added to “Intoxilyzer/Tests,” written by Spencer Hahn. Other authors new that year: Edward LeClaire on Diversion Programs and Conor T. Huseby on Opening Statement. In 2012 an update was published, which provided the excellent contributions of Brad Kalbaugh on felony DUIIs and Shannon Mortimer on diversion.

The tenth edition in 2014 was edited by Bruce Tarbox and he streamlined the ever expanding tome into two parts, the Notebook and the Resource Guide. Other notable changes and new chapters in that edition included ”The Client” chapter by John Powers, a new “Blood, Breath and Urine” by Kenn Meneely and Donald Molnar, and “Violations and Related Charges” by Mark Cogan and Jeff Turnoy. All of the chapters were thoroughly revised and updated, and OCDLA thanks the combined efforts under Bruce Tarbox’s leadership of Colin Andries, Heidi Brockman-Astrue, Russell Barnett, Mark Cogan, Ben Eder, David Eder, Rankin Johnson, Shawn Kollie, Edward Kroll, Edward LeClaire, Lisa Ludwig, Kenn Meneely, Donald Molnar, Shannon Mortimer, Richard Oberdorfer, Joshua Pond, John Powers, Stacey Reding, John Robb, Trace Schreiner, Jason Short, Randall Snow, Bob Thuemmel, Jeff Turnoy, Bear Wilner-Nugent, and L. Todd Wilson.
